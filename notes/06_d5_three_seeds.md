# d=5: long-training models over three model seeds

This note repeats the final-checkpoint analysis of `05_d5_long_training.md` with model seeds 0, 1 and 2 for the four h48/l4 models (static-wide, adaptive-wide, adaptive-stale, static-nominal). adaptive-stale h64/l6 still has seed 0 only. Everything is d=5, 5 rounds, p=0.003, 300k training steps, with the same 4 drift regimes × 5 stream seeds as note 05.

Tables are generated by `python analysis/seeds_d5.py` from existing files and written to `results/exp5/report5_tables.md` and `report5_data.json`. The script imports `analysis/learning_curves.py` and reuses its loading, its pooling over 5 streams and its per-seed McNemar function. It adds mean ± std over model seeds and per-seed tables. The seed-0 rows reproduce note 05 exactly (for example bursts_big drift: static-wide 0.0629, adaptive-wide 0.0516, adaptive-stale 0.0514). Table cells that refer to "report 3" mean note 04 (the 60k-step models) and "report 4" means note 05.

Statistics. "mean ± std" is over the 3 model seeds (sample std, ddof = 1), and each seed is first pooled over 5 stream seeds. Paired tests are exact McNemar on shared shots, run separately for each model seed, in the format of note 05: mean diff, then the largest p over seeds, then (k/n), the number of seeds in which a is lower. Bold means p < 0.05 in every seed and all seeds agree in direction. As in earlier notes, p in 0.001-0.05 counts as weak evidence and there is no multiple-testing correction. McNemar measures shot noise within one model. Variation between seeds shows up only in the std and in the (k/n) count.

## Summary

1. Q1, static-nominal vs MWPM on nominal noise: holds. static-nominal beats MWPM in all 3 seeds: 0.00253 ± 0.00026 vs 0.00329 (largest p = 1e-4). The margin is about 23%, not the 30% that seed 0 suggested; seed 1 is the weakest at 0.00281. It is still not the best model on nominal noise. adaptive-wide beats it in all 3 seeds (−0.00038, largest p = 0.003, weak). static-wide is lower in all 3 seeds, but not significantly in all (largest p = 0.2).
2. Q2, neural vs static MWPM and the best WindowDEM on bursts_big drift: mostly holds. All three wide-trained models beat static MWPM in every seed. adaptive-wide and adaptive-stale also beat the best WindowDEM in every seed. static-wide seed 2 does not: it scores 0.0927 vs 0.0896 for WindowDEM-2000 (+0.0030, p = 4e-5). The spread between seeds is large: static-wide 0.0757 ± 0.0153, adaptive-wide 0.0634 ± 0.0125, adaptive-stale 0.0548 ± 0.0059 (static MWPM 0.0980). Oracle MWPM (0.0426) beats every model in every seed.
3. Q3, adaptive vs oracle on later burst steps: holds in direction, but seed 0 overstated the size on bursts_big. In all 3 seeds both adaptive models beat the oracle on later steps of `bursts` and `hot_qubit`. On `bursts_big` the gap to the oracle shrinks 4.4× for adaptive-wide (+0.0831 → +0.0188) and 6.7× for adaptive-stale (+0.0744 → +0.0111), not the ~11× seen in seed 0. The remaining gap ranges from 0.7 to 3.2 points depending on the seed.
4. Q4, live vs frozen with training: holds, and it is the most reproducible result. Live minus frozen on drift steps barely changes between seeds (std ≤ 0.0014). On bursts_big it shrinks a little in all 3 seeds (adaptive-wide −0.0277 → −0.0221). On bursts and hot_qubit it stays the same, and on telegraph it stays ≈ 0 (−0.0002). adaptive-wide's after-burst regression on bursts_big appears in all 3 seeds (+0.0021 to +0.0024 vs static MWPM). adaptive-stale does not have it.
5. Q5, h64/l6 vs h48/l4: cannot be upgraded. h64/l6 still has one seed. It beats each h48/l4 seed and sits below the whole h48/l4 range on all four measures. But on bursts_big drift the h48/l4 seeds range from 0.0513 to 0.0617, which is wider than h64/l6's lead over the best h48/l4 seed (0.0030). The seed-0 verdict stands: a small gain at 2.2× the GPU time, not clearly worth it.

New from 3 seeds: results on bursts_big drift depend strongly on the training seed. adaptive-stale is the most reliable model: it has the best mean and the smallest spread, and beats static-wide in every seed. adaptive-wide beats static-wide on bursts_big in only 2 of 3 seeds; its seed 1 (0.0765) loses to static-wide seed 1 (0.0716). On the other regimes the ranking is stable across seeds.

## Training runs

All 44 jobs finished: 20 full evals of adaptive-wide and static-nominal seeds 1 and 2, 4 trainings (static-wide and adaptive-stale, seeds 1 and 2, 300k steps each) and 20 full evals of the 4 new models. All 5 models × their seeds × 5 stream seeds have JSONs in `results/exp4/full/st0-st4`. adaptive-stale h64/l6 has seed 0 only, by design.

- The trainings ran 2 at a time (one adaptive-stale with one static-wide), not 4. In a smaller trial run, 4 at once ran out of GPU memory at the val step.
- Each run took 8377-8388 s, about 71.6 steps/s for the pair. A single job alone in note 05 took ≈ 3860 s for 300k steps (≈ 78 steps/s), so running them one after another would have saved about 20 min.
- Every training command is the seed-0 command of note 05 with only `--seed` and `--out` changed. Logs are in `results/exp5/`.

## Answers with 3 seeds

All numbers are for the final 300k checkpoints, pooled over 5 streams. Adaptive models use the live fingerprint unless marked otherwise. Per-seed tables are in `results/exp5/report5_tables.md`, section "Per-seed numbers behind the answers".

Q1. Does static-nominal beat MWPM on nominal noise? Yes, in every seed. Nominal test LER is 0.00232 / 0.00281 / 0.00245 for seeds 0 / 1 / 2, against 0.00329 for MWPM. The mean diff is −0.00076 (largest p = 1e-4, 3/3). It also beats MWPM on the nominal steps of every stream in all seeds (about 0.0024 vs 0.0033). static-nominal is the least stable model across seeds: on the scale ×2 and 2-hot-qubit tests, seed 1 is worse than MWPM (0.02394 vs 0.02265). The wide-trained models remain better on nominal noise. adaptive-wide vs static-nominal is −0.00038 (largest p = 0.003, 3/3: weak but consistent). static-wide vs static-nominal is −0.00028 (lower in 3/3, largest p = 0.2). Seed 0's "static-wide is better, p = 0.04" therefore does not hold up as a significant result. adaptive-wide and static-wide still tie on nominal (−0.00011, p = 0.4).

Q2. Does any neural decoder beat static MWPM and the best WindowDEM on bursts_big drift? Yes, but not every model in every seed.

| model | drift LER (mean ± std) | seeds beating static MWPM (0.0980) | seeds beating best WindowDEM (0.0896) |
|---|---|---|---|
| adaptive-stale | 0.0548 ± 0.0059 | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| adaptive-wide | 0.0634 ± 0.0125 | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| static-wide | 0.0757 ± 0.0153 | 3/3 | 2/3 (seed 2: 0.0927, +0.0030, p = 4e-5) |
| static-nominal | 0.2159 ± 0.0604 | 0/3 | 0/3 |
| h64/l6 (seed 0 only) | 0.0484 | 1/1 | 1/1 |

The best WindowDEM is still picked after the fact (window 2000), which favours it. Oracle MWPM (0.0426) beats every model in every seed. In note 04 every one of these models lost to static MWPM here. Training for 300k steps improves drift LER over the 60k-step models in every seed, every model and every regime (all bold in the "300k vs report 3" rows).

Q3. Does the adaptive model close the gap to the oracle on later burst steps? On `bursts` and `hot_qubit`, yes, in every seed. Later-step diff vs oracle, adaptive-wide / adaptive-stale (all seeds lower):

- bursts: −0.0018 / −0.0021 (largest p = 0.009 / 1e-4)
- hot_qubit: −0.0026 / −0.0025 (p < 1e-100)

On `bursts_big` the gap shrinks a lot but stays open in every seed:

| model | report 3 (60k), mean of 3 seeds | 300k, mean of 3 seeds | 300k, per seed (0 / 1 / 2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| adaptive-wide | +0.0831 | +0.0188 (4.4× smaller) | +0.0071 / +0.0318 / +0.0176 |
| adaptive-stale | +0.0744 | +0.0111 (6.7× smaller) | +0.0075 / +0.0077 / +0.0179 |

Seed 0 was the best seed for adaptive-wide. Its ~11× shrink (+0.0817 → +0.0071) is not typical.

Q4. Does the live vs frozen gap grow, shrink or stay the same with training? It stays about the same; on bursts_big it shrinks slightly. Live − frozen on drift steps, 60k → 300k steps, mean of 3 seeds (all 3/3 seeds, all p < 1e-100 except telegraph, where the largest p = 0.03-0.04 and the 2-SE rule calls it a tie):

| model | bursts | bursts_big | hot_qubit | telegraph |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| adaptive-wide | −0.0107 → −0.0100 | −0.0277 → −0.0221 | −0.0031 → −0.0034 | −0.0003 → −0.0002 (tie) |
| adaptive-stale | −0.0061 → −0.0065 | −0.0135 → −0.0125 | −0.0024 → −0.0025 | −0.0002 → −0.0002 (tie) |

The bursts_big gap shrinks in every adaptive-wide seed (−0.0238 → −0.0213, −0.0309 → −0.0237, −0.0283 → −0.0212). Both live and frozen LERs fall, so the ratio frozen/live on bursts_big drift grows for adaptive-wide, from 1.22 (0.1560 / 0.1283) to 1.35 (0.0854 / 0.0634). The live fingerprint is still worth 1-2 points of LER on large bursts.

The after-burst cost appears in every seed. On bursts_big "after" steps, adaptive-wide is worse than static MWPM by +0.0021 to +0.0024 (p ≤ 6e-11) and worse than its frozen self by +0.0034 to +0.0039. adaptive-stale is not worse than static MWPM (−0.0003 to −0.0006, p = 0.02-0.2). It is still slightly worse than its frozen self (+0.0006 to +0.0009, 3/3).

Q5. Does h64/l6 beat h48/l4 at the same training budget? There is still only one h64/l6 seed. Here it is against each h48/l4 adaptive-stale seed, paired on shots:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | −0.0031 (p = 8e-14) | −0.0012 (p = 1e-11) | −0.00118 (p = 4e-11) | −0.00009 (p = 0.1) |

h64/l6 seed 0 scores 0.0484 on bursts_big drift, below all three h48/l4 seeds (0.0513-0.0617, mean 0.0548 ± 0.0060). But one h64/l6 seed cannot separate architecture from seed luck when the h48/l4 seeds themselves differ by a full point. It is still far below the preset "clearly won" bar (1 point better than h48/l4 on bursts_big drift), and it costs 2.2× the GPU time. Same verdict as note 05.

## Limitations

- Three model seeds is a small sample. The std has large uncertainty, and the bursts_big spread (up to 1.5 points for static-wide and adaptive-wide) suggests three seeds may not show the full range.
- McNemar p-values measure shot noise within each model. The (k/n) counts and the std are the only evidence about variation between seeds. With n = 3 there is no formal test between seeds.
- Learning curves (and the "still falling" decision) are seed 0 only. Step checkpoints for seeds 1 and 2 exist (every 30k) but have no curve evals.
- h64/l6 has one seed.
- The best WindowDEM is picked per phase after the fact.
- There is no multiple-testing correction, and many comparisons are made.
- static-nominal's spread is very large (bursts_big drift 0.16-0.28), so its mean is not a good summary.

## Open items

- If bursts_big matters most, use adaptive-stale: best mean, smallest spread, and no after-burst regression.
- The cause of the seed spread on bursts_big (adaptive-wide seed 1, static-wide seed 2) is unknown. Curve evals of the existing seed-1 and seed-2 step checkpoints (80 evals, no training) would show whether those seeds learn more slowly or settle at a worse point.
- Training is probably not finished on bursts_big (seed 0 was still falling at 300k), so longer training would help more than an h64/l6 network.
