# Report 6: three-seed ensembles at d=5 and d=3

This report follows report 5. Report 5 is now `notes/06_d5_three_seeds.md`: the last commit moved it there from `results/report5.md`. Nothing was trained for this report. `run6.sh` ran 40 ensemble evals (exit 0, 28 min).

Every number here comes from one of three places:

- `results/exp6/report6_tables.md`, generated by `results/exp6/report6.py`.
- `results/exp5/report5_tables.md`, for report 5's single-seed numbers.
- `results/exp6/report6_data.json`. I used it only to see which seed is best or worst in each cell, and then took that seed's row from the tables.

No new evals, no new statistics.

## Setup

- **Ensemble.** The ensemble averages the logits of model seeds 0, 1 and 2 and flips if the mean is > 0. This rule was fixed before the run. There is one ensemble per model type.
- **Fingerprints.** In a live adaptive ensemble, each member keeps its own fingerprint buffer. In a frozen ensemble, every member gets the frozen nominal fingerprint. The fingerprint updates every 200 shots.
- **d=5 models.** The 300k-step h48/l4 models of report 5 (5 rounds, p=0.003).
- **d=3 models.** The 60k-step h48/l4 models of notes 02/03 (3 rounds, p=0.003). They were trained for **5× fewer steps** than the d=5 models. Any d=3 vs d=5 difference therefore mixes distance with training length.
- **Data.** Streams and test sets are the same as in those reports: 4 regimes × 5 stream seeds, pooled, and 200000 shots per test set.
- **Reproducibility check.** Each member run inside the ensemble reproduces its saved single-seed bits with 0 differing shots. The one exception is d=5 adaptive-wide, where 1 of 37,800,000 shots differs.
- **Statistics.** Tests are exact two-sided McNemar on shared shots.
  - p ≤ 0.001 is strong evidence. 0.001 < p < 0.05 is weak evidence, marked *weak*. p ≥ 0.05 is no evidence, marked *n.s.*
  - The tables print p to one significant figure, so "p=0.001" means anything from 0.00095 to 0.0015. I count it as weak.
  - There is no multiple-testing correction.
- **Ensemble vs its own members.** "ens − seed k" compares the ensemble with one of its own members. It is a valid paired test of two decoders on the same shots, but the two decoders are not independent.
- **Best WindowDEM.** The best window is picked per phase after the fact, which favours it.
- Negative diffs mean the first decoder (usually the ensemble) is better.

## Short answers

1. **Does ensembling help?**
   - **d=5: yes, clearly.** On drift steps, every wide-trained ensemble beats all three of its members in all 4 regimes, including the best member. This covers static-wide, adaptive-wide and adaptive-stale, live and frozen (3/3 seeds each, largest p = 1e-07).
     - The gain is largest where seeds disagree most. On bursts_big drift, the ensemble vs its best seed is −0.0065 (static-wide), −0.0032 (adaptive-wide) and −0.0056 (adaptive-stale). Vs the seed mean it is −0.0193, −0.0149 and −0.0091.
     - Ensembling does nothing on after-burst steps: no significant difference vs the best seed anywhere.
     - On nominal steps it gives a small, consistent gain of about −0.0001.
   - **d=3: a little.** The seeds already agree closely: drift std ≤ 0.0008 except on bursts_big, where it is 0.0026–0.0055. Ensemble minus seed mean on drift is between −0.0001 and −0.0069 in 11 of 12 cells for the wide-trained models.
     - The ensemble beats its best seed with strong evidence in only five cells: static-wide on bursts and bursts_big, adaptive-wide on bursts_big and hot_qubit, and adaptive-stale on bursts.
     - Elsewhere it ties the best seed, with one exception where it is worse. static-wide on hot-qubit noise: hot_qubit drift +0.0015 vs seed 0 (p=4e-137), and the 2-hot-qubit test +0.00142 (p=5e-28).
   - **static-nominal (both distances).** The ensemble beats the seed mean but loses to its best seed on bursts and bursts_big. Averaging in a bad seed costs accuracy.
2. **Does adaptive still beat static once both are ensembles? Yes.** The adaptive-stale ensemble beats the static-wide ensemble on drift, onset and later steps in every regime at both distances.
   - All drift p ≤ 3e-29. Three onset cells are only weak: d=5 hot_qubit p=0.004, d=5 telegraph p=0.04, d=3 hot_qubit p=0.005.
   - At d=5 the margin is smaller than with single seeds, because static-wide gains more from ensembling. On bursts_big drift it is −0.0107, against −0.0209 for single seeds in report 5.
   - The exception is the after-burst step. At d=5 there is no difference on bursts (p=0.08), and adaptive-stale is slightly worse on bursts_big (+0.0006, p=0.001, weak). At d=3 adaptive-stale is clearly worse: +0.0013 on bursts and +0.0033 on bursts_big.
3. **Is adaptation still significant with ensembles? Yes, and its size is unchanged.**
   - d=5, live − frozen on bursts_big drift: −0.0196 (adaptive-wide) and −0.0119 (adaptive-stale), p ≤ 3e-247. Report 5's single-seed means were −0.0221 and −0.0125.
   - d=3: −0.0891 and −0.0519.
   - On telegraph the difference stays at 0.0002 or less.
4. **What changes from report 5:**
   - (a) Every wide-trained ensemble now beats the best WindowDEM on bursts_big drift. In report 5, static-wide seed 2 did not.
   - (b) On bursts_big later steps, the gap to oracle MWPM shrinks to +0.0039 (adaptive-wide) and +0.0019 (adaptive-stale), from +0.0188 and +0.0111. It is still significant.
   - (c) The static-wide ensemble now beats oracle MWPM on hot_qubit drift (−0.0005, p=2e-06).
   - (d) The h48/l4 ensembles match or beat the single h64/l6 seed on bursts_big drift: adaptive-stale ensemble 0.0457, adaptive-wide ensemble 0.0484, h64/l6 0.0484. There is no paired test for this.
   - Unchanged: oracle MWPM still beats every ensemble on bursts_big drift, and ensembling does not fix adaptive-wide's after-burst regression.
   - New at d=3: the adaptive-wide ensemble beats oracle MWPM on later bursts steps (−0.0018, p=3e-05), where single seeds tied it in note 03.
5. **Best ensemble and baseline.**
   - **d=5: the adaptive-stale ensemble is best overall.** It is lowest on bursts_big drift (0.0457), about equal to adaptive-wide on bursts (0.0170 vs 0.0171) and telegraph (0.0164 vs 0.0164), slightly behind on hot_qubit (0.0073 vs 0.0071), and has half of adaptive-wide's after-burst cost.
   - **d=3:** the adaptive-stale and adaptive-wide ensembles trade places by phase.
   - **Baseline for the new fingerprint:** the d=3 adaptive-stale ensemble, with the adaptive-wide ensemble as a second reference. The numbers are in section 5.
   - No paired test between the adaptive-stale and adaptive-wide ensembles was computed, so this ranking rests on LER alone.

## Limitations

- **One trio per model type.** Each model type has one ensemble, built from one set of 3 seeds. There is no spread over ensembles, so we do not know how a different trio would do. That matters most at d=5, where single seeds span 0.0629–0.0927 (static-wide) and 0.0516–0.0765 (adaptive-wide) on bursts_big drift.
- **Compute.** An ensemble costs 3× the inference compute of one model, and no latency was measured here. Ensemble vs single model is not compute-matched, and neither is ensemble vs h64/l6 (2.2× the training GPU time of h48/l4 in report 5).
- **Not independent.** Each ensemble contains the members it is compared with, so those tests compare related decoders.
- **Missing pairs.** Several ensemble-vs-ensemble pairs were not computed by `report6.py`:
  - adaptive-stale vs adaptive-wide
  - adaptive-wide vs static-wide
  - any ensemble vs the static-nominal ensemble
  - any ensemble vs h64/l6

  The rankings between these rest on unpaired LERs.
- **Batch size.** Batch size changes ensemble results slightly. In the batch-size test (`results/exp6/concurrency.txt`), the d=5 adaptive-wide ensemble's stream-0 bursts_big drift LER was 0.0572 at batch 196608 and 0.0571 at batch 16384, the batch used for this report. This is far below the effects reported here. It is not zero, so record the batch size with any result.
- **One differing shot.** 1 shot of 37,800,000 differs between the d=5 adaptive-wide members and their saved single-seed bits. The cause was not checked.
- **Distance mixed with training length.** d=3 models had 60k training steps and d=5 models 300k. Smaller ensembling gains at d=3 may come from training length, not distance.
- **Unexplained regression.** The d=3 static-wide hot-qubit regression under ensembling is unexplained.
- **Best WindowDEM** is picked per phase after the fact.
- **Multiple testing.** Many tests, no multiple-testing correction.

## Claims for the paper

1. **Neural decoders trained on wide noise beat static MWPM and the best tuned WindowDEM on drifting noise at d=5, including large bursts.** *Holds.* All three ensembles beat both on bursts_big drift with p < 1e-300. With single seeds this failed in 1 of 9 model-seeds.
2. **A live-fingerprint decoder beats a static decoder trained on the same data, on drift steps of every regime, at d=3 and d=5.** *Holds.* With ensembles on both sides, all drift p ≤ 3e-29.
3. **Size of that advantage at d=5.** *Weakened.* With ensembles on both sides it is about half the single-seed margin on bursts_big drift (−0.0107 vs −0.0209). It is also smaller on bursts (−0.0053 vs −0.0074), hot_qubit (−0.0025 vs −0.0029) and telegraph (−0.0010 vs −0.0015).
4. **The advantage on localized drift comes from adaptation: live vs frozen fingerprint, same weights.** *Holds.* Ensembling leaves live − frozen almost unchanged (d=5 bursts_big −0.0196 / −0.0119).
5. **Uniform scale drift (telegraph) gets no benefit from adaptation.** *Holds as a practical statement* (≤ 0.0002). *Weakened as a "tie"*: at d=5 the ensembles show a significant −0.0002 (p=0.0002 adaptive-stale, p=0.002 adaptive-wide).
6. **Adaptive decoders beat oracle MWPM on later steps of moderate bursts and hot-qubit drift at d=5.** *Holds*, with stronger p (bursts later −0.0034, p=2e-34 for adaptive-stale).
7. **Oracle MWPM is still better on large bursts.** *Holds*, but the gap is now small: +0.0019 on bursts_big later steps for the adaptive-stale ensemble (p=2e-05), against +0.0111 for single seeds.
8. **Stale-fingerprint augmentation removes the after-burst regression at d=5.** *Holds.* Adaptive-stale ensemble −0.0007 vs static MWPM (weak). Adaptive-wide ensemble +0.0021 (3e-11). *New:* ensembling does not reduce the after-burst cost for any model.
9. **A 3-seed logit-averaged ensemble beats every one of its members on drift steps at d=5 and removes most of the seed dependence seen in report 5.** *New.*
10. **At d=3 (60k-step models) ensembling gains are small, and ensembling can hurt (static-wide on hot-qubit noise).** *New.*
11. **At d=3 the adaptive-wide ensemble beats oracle MWPM on later steps of moderate bursts** (−0.0018, p=3e-05; single seeds tied). *New*, single regime.
12. **The static-wide ensemble beats oracle MWPM on hot-qubit drift at d=5** (−0.0005, p=2e-06). *New*, small effect.
13. **A trained static decoder beats MWPM on nominal noise.** *Holds.* The static-nominal ensemble is at −0.00108 (p=3e-23).
14. **A 3-seed h48/l4 ensemble matches a larger h64/l6 network on bursts_big drift** (0.0457 / 0.0484 vs 0.0484). *New*, but weak: unpaired, and h64/l6 has one seed.
