# d=5: multiscale fingerprint (variant B) vs the adaptive-stale ensemble

This note tests note 08's variant B (multiscale fingerprint) at d=5 against the d=5 adaptive-stale ensemble of note 07. It applies the same decision rule that was fixed in advance for round 7. Variant C (multiscale+pairs) was not run at d=5.

Every number here comes from `results/exp8/report8_tables.md` (generated by `results/exp8/report8.py`) and `results/exp8/STATUS.txt`, plus notes 07 and 08 for earlier results. Nothing was trained or evaluated for this note. I did not read `check8.txt`, `report8_data.json` or the logs. Where I give a relative size that the tables do not print, I compute it from the rounded LERs in the tables and mark it "≈".

## Setup

- **Models.** All are d=5 models: 5 rounds, p=0.003, `data/d5_wide_x5`, h48/l4, fp32, batch 512, 300000 steps, `--compile`, model seeds 0, 1 and 2.
- **Baseline (adaptive-stale).** The existing adaptive-stale models: `--stale-prob 0.3`, single fingerprint.
- **B multiscale.** The same command with `--fingerprint multiscale`. It has three fingerprint channels:
  - long = the whole 2000-shot window
  - short = the last 200 shots
  - short − long
- **static-wide.** The existing static models trained on `data/d5_wide_x5`.
- **Reused results.** Baseline and static-wide results are reused from `results/exp6`, not re-run.
- **Ensemble.** The mean of the 3 seeds' logits, thresholded at > 0. This rule was fixed in advance, as in notes 07 and 08.
- **Live vs frozen.** Live: each model's own causal fingerprint buffer. Frozen: every channel comes from the nominal test set's fingerprint shots.
- **Streams.** The fingerprint updates every 200 shots. Streams are pooled over stream seeds 0–4.
- **Statistics.**
  - Exact two-sided McNemar on shared shots. diff = LER(a) − LER(b), so negative means a is better.
  - **Bold = p < 0.001**, which is the rule's threshold. p from 0.001 to 0.05 is weak evidence.
  - Following note 07, a printed "p=0.001" counts as weak.
  - There is no multiple-testing correction.
  - The best WindowDEM is picked per phase after the fact.

The multiscale gains shrink by an order of magnitude or vanish from d=3 to d=5. On bursts_big the gains turn into losses, and on nominal steps a small gain becomes a significant cost. Several explanations fit these files, and none of them is tested:

- **Less headroom on bursts.** At d=5 the baseline already tied oracle MWPM at bursts onset (−0.0001, p=0.9). At d=3 it was +0.0219 above oracle.

- **Confounds.** d=3 and d=5 also differ in training length (60k vs 300k steps) and training data (`d3_wide` vs `d5_wide_x5`). The distance effect cannot be separated from these.

## Limitations

- **One trio per variant.** There is no spread over ensembles. The baseline and static-wide trios are the note 07 trios.
- **Reused baseline results.** Baseline and static-wide results come from `results/exp6`. The sources used here do not record the eval batch size. Note 07 showed that batch size can move an ensemble LER by about 0.0001.
- **No channel check reported.** I did not read `check8.txt`, so this note does not report the fingerprint channel checks for d=5.
- **The rule counts any loss with p < 0.001, whatever its size.** At d=5 the deciding losses are 4–5% relative. They are not marginal cells.
- **Approximate relative values.** Relative sizes marked "≈" are computed from LERs rounded to 4 decimals. They are rough for small LERs such as after-burst and nominal steps.
- **Unpaired comparisons.** Frozen-vs-frozen comparisons and seed means are unpaired.
- **Statistics.** McNemar measures shot noise only. There is no multiple-testing correction. The best WindowDEM is picked per phase after the fact.

## Open items

- **Find the seed behind the nominal-step cost.** Use the per-seed data in `report8_data.json` (no new evals) to find which B seed causes the nominal-step regression and the large nominal std.
- **Check whether the nominal-step cost is short-channel residue.** Look at B's short and short − long channels on nominal steps that follow drift. This needs the channel values, not new training.
- **Explain the bursts_big onset result.** At d=5, B gives no gain at bursts_big onset even though there is +0.0065 of headroom to oracle.
- **Decide what to do with B.** Under the rule as written, B fails at both distances. Any change to the rule must be written down before the next run and not applied to this one. An example would be a relative-size floor for losses, or adding nominal steps to the no-loss condition.
- **Variant C at d=5 has not been run.** Note 08's covariance-range hypothesis is still untested.

## Claims for the paper

### Holds

1. **Wide-trained neural decoders beat static MWPM and the best tuned WindowDEM on drifting noise at d=5, including large bursts.** B also does this, in every phase of every regime (p ≤ 2e-07).
2. **A live-fingerprint decoder beats a static decoder trained on the same data, on drift steps of every regime at d=5.** This now holds for two fingerprint designs. B vs static-wide on drift: −0.0057, −0.0088, −0.0023 and −0.0010 (p ≤ 5e-28).
3. **The advantage on localized drift comes from adaptation.** For B, live − frozen on bursts_big drift is −0.0145 (p=2e-309).
4. **Uniform scale drift (telegraph) gets no practical benefit from adaptation.** B's live − frozen is ≤ 0.0005 in absolute value, and B ties the baseline on telegraph.
5. **Adaptive decoders beat oracle MWPM on later steps of moderate bursts and on hot-qubit drift at d=5.** B: bursts later −0.0033 (2e-32), hot_qubit drift −0.0028 (2e-202).
6. **Oracle MWPM is still better on large bursts at d=5.** This holds for B, with a larger gap than the baseline's (bursts_big later +0.0044 vs +0.0019).
7. **Stale-fingerprint training keeps the after-burst step at or below static MWPM at d=5.** B is −0.0012 (2e-10) and −0.0011 (2e-07), which is stronger than the baseline.
8. **The single-fingerprint adaptive-stale ensemble is the d=5 model to beat.** B fails the pre-set rule against it.
9. **Richer fingerprints lean more on the fingerprint: a larger live − frozen gap and a weaker frozen decoder.** This was seen at d=3 (note 08) and is seen again for B at d=5. The frozen comparison is unpaired.

### Weakened

10. **"A multiscale fingerprint fixes onset lag"** (note 08, d=3). At d=5 the gain is −0.0019 (≈ −10%) on bursts onset only. bursts_big onset (+0.0003, p=0.7) and hot_qubit onset (+0.0001, p=1) show none. This cannot be stated as a general result. The d=3/d=5 difference is also confounded with training length and data.
11. **"The multiscale fingerprint removes the after-burst cost"** (d=3). At d=5 B's gain over the baseline after bursts is only weak (−0.0003, p=0.02; −0.0005, p=0.002). Against static MWPM it is strong, but the baseline was already below static MWPM at d=5.
12. **"B nearly passes the rule"** (d=3: one +0.0002 hot_qubit cell, about 1%). This does not carry over to d=5. There B fails on bursts_big drift (+4.1%) and later (+5.4%) at p ≤ 4e-09.

### New

13. **At d=5, B is worse than the single-fingerprint baseline on large bursts.** It loses on bursts_big drift (+0.0019, +4.1%, p=4e-09) and later steps (+0.0024, +5.4%, p=2e-11). Its gap to oracle on bursts_big later steps more than doubles (+0.0019 → +0.0044).
14. **The B ensemble beats oracle MWPM at bursts onset at d=5** (−0.0020, p=0.0002). It is the first d=5 model in these notes to do so; the baseline tied oracle there. This holds in one regime only. bursts_big onset is still +0.0068 above oracle.
15. **At d=5 the live multiscale fingerprint costs accuracy on nominal stream steps.** The cost is +6.4% to +17.7% relative (p ≤ 1e-10) in all four regimes. It is not seen on the nominal test set (−0.00002, p=0.7) or with a frozen fingerprint (identical LERs). The most likely source is leftover drift in the fingerprint buffer. One seed may drive most of it (nominal std 0.0003–0.0007 against ≤ 0.0001). Neither point has been checked.
16. **On the d=5 test sets, the multiscale and single fingerprints give the same accuracy** (all p ≥ 0.1).
